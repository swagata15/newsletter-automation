# Research notes: Do people trust AI more than humans? Algorithm appreciation and aversion
All figures from the primary papers' abstracts/results or the official report page, read 2026-11-05.

## [1] University of Melbourne and KPMG (2025), Trust, attitudes and use of Artificial Intelligence: A global study 2025
URL: https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ai-and-technology/trust-attitudes-and-use-of-ai.html
- Led by the University of Melbourne (Prof. Nicole Gillespie, Dr Steve Lockey) with KPMG; surveyed more than 48,000 people across 47 countries.
- 66% of people use AI regularly; 83% believe the use of AI will result in a wide range of benefits.
- "only 46% of people globally are willing to trust AI systems"
- 70% believe regulation is needed.
- "Many rely on AI output without evaluating accuracy (66%) and are making mistakes in their work due to AI (56%)."

## [2] Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey (2015), Algorithm Aversion: People Erroneously Avoid Algorithms after Seeing Them Err, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General
URL: https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033
- "people are especially averse to algorithmic forecasters after seeing them perform, even when they see them outperform a human forecaster. This is because people more quickly lose confidence in algorithmic than human forecasters after seeing them make the same mistake."
- 5 studies; participants who saw the algorithm perform were less likely to choose it over an inferior human forecaster.

## [3] Logg, Minson & Moore (2019), Algorithm appreciation: People prefer algorithmic to human judgment, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.005
- "results from six experiments show that lay people adhere more to advice when they think it comes from an algorithm than from a person."
- Appreciation waned when people chose between an algorithm's estimate and their own, and when they had expertise.
- "Paradoxically, experienced professionals, who make forecasts on a regular basis, relied less on algorithmic advice than lay people did, which hurt their accuracy."

## [4] Ayers et al. (2023), Comparing Physician and Artificial Intelligence Chatbot Responses to Patient Questions Posted to a Public Social Media Forum, JAMA Internal Medicine
URL: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2023.1838
- 195 exchanges from Reddit's r/AskDocs (October 2022); chatbot (ChatGPT) answers generated Dec 2022; evaluated in triplicate by licensed health care professionals, blinded.
- "evaluators preferred chatbot responses to physician responses in 78.6% (95% CI, 75.0%-81.8%) of the 585 evaluations."
- Physician responses 52 words vs chatbot 211 words (median).
- Good or very good quality: chatbot 78.5%, physicians 22.1% (3.6 times higher).
- Empathetic or very empathetic: chatbot 45.1%, physicians 4.6% (9.8 times higher).

## [5] Longoni, Bonezzi & Morewedge (2019), Resistance to Medical Artificial Intelligence, Journal of Consumer Research
URL: https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucz013
- "Consumers are reluctant to utilize healthcare provided by AI in real and hypothetical choices"
- Driver: "Uniqueness neglect, a concern that AI providers are less able than human providers to account for consumers' unique characteristics and circumstances"
- Resistance eliminated when AI care is framed as personalized, or "only supports, rather than replaces, a decision made by a human healthcare provider".
