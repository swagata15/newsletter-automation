# Research notes: Growing up with AI, kids' development
Read 2026-11-05.

## [1] Pew Research Center (Feb 24, 2026), How Teens Use and View AI
URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/ (PDF: https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/02/PI_2026.02.24_Teens-and-AI_REPORT.pdf)
- Surveyed 1,458 U.S. teens and their parents online, Sept. 25 to Oct. 9, 2025.
- "While about half of parents say their teen uses chatbots, higher shares of teens themselves (64%) report using them."
- "When asked about their own teen, 51% of parents say their teen uses chatbots, while 18% say they do not."
- Used chatbots to search for information (57%), get help with schoolwork (54%), for fun or entertainment (47%), casual conversations (16%), emotional support (12%).
- "1 in 10 teens say they do all or most schoolwork with chatbots' help"; some (21%) or a little (23%).
- "A majority of teens (59%) think using AI to cheat is a regular occurrence at their school"
- Among teens using chatbots for schoolwork, 76% say students at their school use chatbots to cheat at least sometimes.

## [2] Common Sense Media (2026), Common Sense Census: AI Use by Tweens and Teens (ages 9 to 17)
URL (coverage): https://weartv.com/news/nation-world/nearly-9-in-10-kids-use-ai-report-flags-concerns-over-educational-emotional-dependency-common-sense-media-youth-ai-safety-institute-common-sense-media-census-ai-use-by-tweens-and-teens-2026
- "86% of kids have used generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT and Google's Gemini."
- "81% of 9- to 12-year-olds, 89% of 13- to 15-year-olds, and 92% of 16- to 17-year-olds use some form of generative AI."
- Among kids who use AI, 89% use it for fun or entertainment; more than eight in 10 for schoolwork (94% of older teens).
- "over half, 57%, said they turn to AI for advice on their health or body."
- "nearly half, 44%, of kids haven't had a conversation with their parents about AI safety."
- "A fifth of the kids who use AI, including 42% of the kids who use AI on a daily basis, said it would be hard to go without AI for a month."
- "Among kids who use AI for homework help, 16% said they have difficulty starting or completing their homework when they can't use AI."

## [3] Xu, Aubele, Vigil, Bustamante, Kim & Warschauer (2022), Dialogue with a conversational agent promotes children's story comprehension via enhancing engagement, Child Development
URL: https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13708
- 117 three- to six-year-olds randomly assigned: dialogic or non-dialogic reading, with an agent or a human.
- "a conversational agent can replicate the benefits of dialogic reading with a human partner by enhancing children's narrative-relevant vocalizations, reducing irrelevant vocalizations, and improving story comprehension."

## [4] Wojcik, Prasad, Hutchinson & Shen (2022), Children prefer to learn from smart devices, but do not trust them more than humans, International Journal of Child-Computer Interaction
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcci.2021.100406

## [5] Kurian (2024), 'No, Alexa, no!': designing child-safe AI and protecting children from the risks of the 'empathy gap' in large language models, Learning, Media and Technology
URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/17439884.2024.2367052
- Conceptualises the risk of LLMs for children as "an empathy gap"; "the need to design LLMs that prevent or mitigate against the risks of responding inappropriately to children's personal disclosures or accidentally promoting harm."
- Eight dimensions of child-safe design and policy, including human intervention, transparency and school-family engagement.

## [6] Bastani et al. (2025), PNAS, Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning
URL: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
- High school students with plain GPT-4 access during practice did 17% worse on the exam than students without access; a hint-giving tutor largely mitigated the harm.
