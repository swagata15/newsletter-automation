# Research notes: Does using AI make us worse at thinking?
All figures read from the full papers or official abstracts on 2026-11-05.

## [1] Kosmyna et al. (2025), MIT Media Lab, "Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872 (preprint, under review)
- 54 participants in Sessions 1-3, 18 completed session 4. Three groups: LLM, Search Engine, Brain-only. EEG during essay writing.
- Abstract: "Brain-only participants exhibited the strongest, most distributed networks; Search Engine users showed moderate engagement; and LLM users displayed the weakest connectivity."
- Abstract: "Self-reported ownership of essays was the lowest in the LLM group and the highest in the Brain-only group. LLM users also struggled to accurately quote their own work."
- Abstract: "Over four months, LLM users consistently underperformed at neural, linguistic, and behavioral levels."
- Session 1 quoting: "In the LLM-assisted group, 83.3 % of participants (15/18) failed to provide a correct quotation, whereas only 11.1 % (2/18) in both the Search-Engine and Brain-Only groups encountered the same difficulty." LLM group significantly worse (p < .001).
- Critique: Stankovic et al., "Comment on: Your Brain on ChatGPT" https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00856 raises concerns including limited sample size, reproducibility, EEG methodology, and inconsistent reporting.

## [2] Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakci & Mariman (2025), PNAS, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics"
URL: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122 (published 2025-06-25); working paper https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486
- Field experiment, nearly a thousand high school math students in Turkey (Fall 2023). Two tutors: GPT Base (standard ChatGPT interface) and GPT Tutor (prompts designed to safeguard learning: gives hints, knows the solution and common mistakes).
- "access to GPT-4 significantly improves performance (48% improvement for GPT Base and 127% for GPT Tutor)"
- "when access is subsequently taken away, students actually perform worse than those who never had access (17% reduction for GPT Base)"
- "while students in the GPT Tutor arm did not perform better on the exam (relative to the control arm), they perceived that they performed significantly better"
- "While GPT Tutor appears to largely mitigate these negative effects"
- Students used GPT-4 as a "crutch" during practice.

## [3] Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks & Wilson (2025), CHI 2025, Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon
Title: The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers
URL: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/ (PDF: https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)
- Surveyed 319 knowledge workers; 936 first-hand examples of using GenAI at work.
- Abstract: "Specifically, higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking."
- Abstract: "GenAI shifts the nature of critical thinking toward information verification, response integration, and task stewardship."
- "We find that GenAI tools reduce the perceived effort of critical thinking while also encouraging over-reliance on AI, with confidence in the tool often diminishing independent problem-solving."
- 309 of 319 (96.87%) used ChatGPT.

## [4] Gerlich (2025), Societies 15(1), 6, "AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking"
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006 (published 2025-01-03)
- Surveys and in-depth interviews with 666 participants.
- "The findings revealed a significant negative correlation between frequent AI tool usage and critical thinking abilities, mediated by increased cognitive offloading."
- "Younger participants exhibited higher dependence on AI tools and lower critical thinking scores compared to older participants."
- "higher educational attainment was associated with better critical thinking skills, regardless of AI usage."
- Correlational design.

## [5] Sparrow, Liu & Wegner (2011), Science, "Google Effects on Memory: Cognitive Consequences of Having Information at Our Fingertips"
URL: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1207745
- Abstract: "when people expect to have future access to information, they have lower rates of recall of the information itself and enhanced recall instead for where to access it."
- "The Internet has become a primary form of external or transactive memory"
