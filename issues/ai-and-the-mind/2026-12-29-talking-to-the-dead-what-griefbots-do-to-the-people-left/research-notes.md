# Research notes: Grief tech, AI recreations of people who have died
Read 2026-11-05.

## [1] Hollanek & Nowaczyk-Basińska (2024), Griefbots, Deadbots, Postmortem Avatars: on Responsible Applications of Generative AI in the Digital Afterlife Industry, Philosophy & Technology (University of Cambridge)
URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-024-00744-w
- Three speculative design scenarios for AI-enabled simulation of the deceased.
- Perspectives: the "data donor" (whose data creates the deadbot), the "data recipient" (holds the data after death), and the "service interactant" (who talks to it).
- Recommendations: "developing sensitive procedures for retiring deadbots, ensuring meaningful transparency, restricting access to such services to adult users only, and adhering to the principle of mutual consent of both data donors and service interactants."

## [2] Xygkou, Siriaraya, Covaci, Prigerson, Neimeyer, Ang et al. (2023), The "Conversation" about Loss: Understanding How Chatbot Technology was Used in Supporting People in Grief, CHI 2023
URL: https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581154 (repository: https://kar.kent.ac.uk/99576)
- Semi-structured interviews with 10 mourners who actively used chatbots at different phases of their loss.
- "seven approaches in which chatbots were used to help people cope with grief, by taking the role of listener, acting as a simulation of the deceased, romantic partner, friend and emotion coach."

## [3] Li & Lan (2026), Remember You: Understanding How Users Use Deadbots to Reconstruct Memories of the Deceased
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01017
- In-depth interviews with 26 users.
- "users are not passive recipients but active constructors of the deceased's digital representation"
- "they build an idealized digital figure blending authentic memories with personal expectations."
- "Deadbots provide a private space to grieve without social pressure and a channel to address unresolved emotions."
- Memory evolves "from initial reinforcement and idealization to a later stage where AI-generated new memories blur with authentic recollections"
- "This blurring raises ethical concerns regarding memory distortion and dependency, underscoring the need for future clinical research on the long-term impact of AI-mediated grieving."

## [4] Morris & Brubaker (2024), Generative Ghosts: Anticipating Benefits and Risks of AI Afterlives (Google DeepMind / University of Colorado)
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01662
- "We anticipate that within our lifetimes it may become common practice for people to create custom AI agents to interact with loved ones and/or the broader world after death"
- "We call these generative ghosts, since such agents will be capable of generating novel content rather than merely parroting content produced by their creator while living."

## [5] Lundorff, Holmgren, Zachariae, Farver-Vestergaard & O'Connor (2017), Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis, Journal of Affective Disorders
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030
- 14 studies; "pooled prevalence of PGD of 9.8% (95% CI 6.8-14.0)"
- "one out of ten bereaved adults is at risk for PGD."
