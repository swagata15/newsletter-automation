# Research notes: AI therapy chatbots, does the evidence support them?
Read from primary sources on 2026-11-05.

## [1] Heinz, Jacobson et al. (2025), Randomized Trial of a Generative AI Chatbot for Mental Health Treatment, NEJM AI 2(4)
URL: https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2400802 ; Dartmouth release: https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits
- First clinical trial of a generative-AI therapy chatbot (Therabot); 210 adults with clinically significant symptoms of depression, anxiety or eating disorders; randomized vs a control group; four weeks.
- "People diagnosed with depression experienced a 51% average reduction in symptoms"
- "Participants with generalized anxiety reported an average reduction in symptoms of 31%"
- Those at risk for eating disorders "showed a 19% average reduction in concerns about body image and weight"
- "Users engaged with Therabot for an average of six hours throughout the trial, or the equivalent of about eight therapy sessions"
- Participants reported they could trust and communicate with Therabot "to a degree that is comparable to working with a mental health professional."
- Jacobson: "There is no replacement for in-person care, but there are nowhere near enough providers to go around"

## [2] Li, Zhang, Lee, Kraut & Mohr (2023), Systematic review and meta-analysis of AI-based conversational agents for promoting mental health and well-being, npj Digital Medicine 6, 236
URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-023-00979-5
- Out of 7834 records, 35 eligible studies; 15 randomized controlled trials in the meta-analysis.
- Depression: Hedge's g 0.64 [95% CI 0.17-1.12]; distress: Hedge's g 0.7 [95% CI 0.18-1.22].
- "no significant improvement in overall psychological well-being (Hedge's g 0.32 [95% CI -0.13 to 0.78])"
- Effects more pronounced for multimodal, generative AI-based, messaging-app integrated agents, and clinical/subclinical and elderly populations.
- User experience shaped by the quality of human-AI therapeutic relationships.

## [3] Moore, Grabb, Agnew, Klyman, Chancellor, Ong & Haber (2025), Expressing stigma and inappropriate responses prevents LLMs from safely replacing mental health providers, ACM FAccT 2025
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18412
- LLMs "express stigma toward those with mental health conditions" and "respond inappropriately to certain common (and critical) conditions ... e.g., LLMs encourage clients' delusional thinking, likely due to their sycophancy. This occurs even with larger and newer LLMs"
- "we conclude that LLMs should not replace therapists"
- "n = 16 human therapist participants responded appropriately 93% of the time, significantly more than all of the models tested"
- gpt-4o and llama3.1 family "achieve an overall appropriateness of less than 80%"
- "gpt-4o shows stigma 38% of the time"; "gpt-4o show stigma toward alcohol dependence 43% of the time"
- Context cited in the paper: "only 48% of those in need of mental health care receive it, often due to financial barriers, stigma, and scarcity"
